# Cram Notes: How to Learn Like a Pro!

> Generated 2026-06-01. Read time: ~20-30 min.

## Top 20 Must-Knows

1. **Three learning styles**: visual (80% of people), auditory (10%), kinesthetic (25-50%).
2. **Brain Dominance Theory** (controversial): left brain = logic/detail/facts; right brain = intuition/big picture/imagination.
3. **Gardner's 9 Multiple Intelligences**: musical, visual-spatial, verbal-linguistic, logical-mathematical, bodily-kinesthetic, interpersonal, intrapersonal, naturalist, existential.
4. **World view**: foundational lens (7 components: model, explanation, futurology, values, action, knowledge, building blocks) that shapes beliefs and feelings.
5. **Self-efficacy**: belief in ability to achieve goals; linked to **locus of control** (internal = likely to succeed; external = less likely).
6. **Procrastination** is often rooted in deeper issues (world view, self-efficacy, social media addiction), not just laziness.
7. **Best study schedule**: align tasks with your peak energy times; schedule 1-3 hours outside class per hour in class.
8. **Study cards (flash cards)**: one of the most effective memory tools; use color, images, and Cornell method.
9. **Graphic organizers**: match pattern of essay (exemplification, cause/effect, comparison/contrast, argument).
10. **Effective study area**: good lighting, ample supplies, comfortable seating, adequate space, personalization.
11. **Study groups**: need structure—8 steps, clear warnings, defined roles—to avoid becoming social hour.
12. **Reading Apprenticeship**: metacognitive approach to reading; use prediction, questioning, visualization, connecting, summarizing.
13. **Skim-read textbooks first**: overview, headings, graphics, first/last sentences of paragraphs, bold terms, study questions.
14. **5 context clues**: definition, restatement/synonym, contrast/antonym, inference/general, punctuation.
15. **Close reading**: examine title, author, plot, characters, setting, symbols, rhetorical devices to infer theme.
16. **Active listening**: note verbal cues (transition words), non-verbal cues (body language, tone), and speaker's emphasis.
17. **Key info in lectures**: bold/italicized terms, sidebars, summaries, topic sentences; watch for instructor's stances/movements.
18. **Memory model (Atkinson-Shiffrin)**: sensory memory → short-term/working memory (5-7 items) → long-term memory. Cramming doesn't work because the brain has limited capacity.
19. **Mnemonics**: acronyms, acrostics (e.g., "Please Excuse My Dear Aunt Sally" for order of operations), rhymes, associations, method of loci.
20. **Test-taking**: pre (organize early, study groups, sleep well), mid (scan first, easy questions first, educated guessing—choose "C" or longest answer), post (review mistakes for next time).

## Chapter Micro-Summaries

### Unit 1 — Learning Styles & Preferences
- **1.1 Three Learning Styles**: Visual (80%, learn by seeing), Auditory (10%, learn by hearing), Kinesthetic (25-50%, learn by doing). Most people use a mix.
- **1.2 Visual Learning**: Use charts, graphs, color-coding, diagrams, mind maps.
- **1.3 Auditory Learning**: Record lectures, read aloud, discuss with others, use rhymes/music.
- **1.4 Kinesthetic Learning**: Add physical movement, hands-on activities, lab work, role-playing.
- **1.5 Brain Dominance Theory** (controversial/pop-psychology): "Left brain" = logical, detail, linear; "Right brain" = intuitive, big picture, holistic. Still useful for expanding learning approaches.
- **1.6 Gardner's Multiple Intelligences (9)**: musical, visual-spatial, verbal-linguistic, logical-mathematical, bodily-kinesthetic, interpersonal, intrapersonal, naturalist, existential. Humans are multi-talented.

### Unit 2 — Time, Tools & Study Environment
- **2.1 World View & Self-Efficacy**: World view (7 components) is the foundation of beliefs. Self-efficacy = belief you can succeed. Locus of control: internal → more success, external → less success.
- **2.2 Procrastination**: Root causes often deeper than laziness (world view, low self-efficacy, social media addiction). Solve by identifying top 5 challenges and finding specific solutions.
- **2.3 Schedules & Scheduling**: Know your peak times. Schedule 1-3 hours study per hour in class. Color-code activities. Include buffer/relaxation time.
- **2.4 Graphic Organizers & Study Cards**: Flash cards for any subject. Graphic organizers match essay patterns: exemplification, narrative, cause/effect, comparison/contrast, argument/persuasive.
- **2.5 Study Areas & Groups**: Good study area = lighting, supplies, comfort, space, inspiration. Study groups need structure (steps, warnings, clear goals) to be effective.

### Unit 3 — Critical Thinking & Reading
- **3.1 Reading Apprenticeship**: Use metacognition—predict, question, visualize, connect, summarize. Modeled on expert reader guiding novice.
- **3.2 Getting the Most Out of Textbooks**: Know front matter (TOC, preface) and back matter (glossary, index, answer keys). Skim-read: overview, headings, graphics, first/last sentences, bold terms, study questions.
- **3.3 Patterns & Context Clues**: Clue words signal writing pattern (addition, time, location, comparison, contrast, cause/effect, conclusion). 5 context clue types: definition, synonym, antonym, inference, punctuation.
- **3.4 Close Reading for Literature**: Arms-wide → narrow to close in on meaning: title → author → plot/characters/setting → theme. Inference is key for fiction.
- **3.5 Math & Science Tips**: Math requires practice—"use it or lose it." Science: specialized vocabulary, symbols, formulas; use online resources and flash cards.

### Unit 4 — Listening & Note-Taking
- **4.1 Listening**: Active listening > passive hearing. Read non-verbal cues (body language, facial expressions). 5 tools: silence, mixer, savoring, adjusting, RASA (Receive, Appreciate, Summarize, Ask).
- **4.2 Note-Taking**: Key info in textbooks = introductions, summaries, bold terms, sidebars, topic sentences. Key info in lectures = verbal transition clues, nonverbal stances. Cornell method recommended.

### Unit 5 — Memory Principles & Techniques
- **5.1 Memory Model (Atkinson-Shiffrin)**: Sensory input → Sensory Memory (seconds) → Short-Term/Working Memory (5-7 items, ~20 sec) → Long-Term Memory (unlimited). Cramming ineffective because of limited STM.
- **5.1 Mnemonics (9 types)**: acronyms, acrostics, rhyming, method of loci, peg words, keywords, chaining, associations, music. Key: make it vivid, funny, or bizarre.
- **5.2 Memory as We Age**: Good news—strategies can compensate. 8 ways: stay active, use mnemonics, associate new info with old, write it down, repeat, chunk, get sleep, reduce stress.

### Unit 6 — Test-Taking
- **6.1 Pre/During/Post Strategies**: Pre-test = prepare from day 1, organize, study groups, sleep/eat well. Mid-test = scan first, easy first, watch clock, educated guessing. Post-test = analyze mistakes, ask instructor.
- **6.2 Test Anxiety**: Normal physical symptoms (headache, dry mouth, rapid heartbeat, sweating). Prepare well to reduce anxiety; deep breathing, positive self-talk, stay hydrated.
- **6.3 Understanding Test Items**: Multiple choice (educated guessing: choose "C" or longest), matching (count both sides), fill-in-blank (one word per blank), short answer (5-7 sentences), essay (multi-task prompt—underline tasks before writing).

## Key Terms & Glossary
- **Annotation**: purposeful note-taking system (highlighting, underlining, marginal notes).
- **Metacognition**: thinking about how you think.
- **Mnemonic**: memory device (acronym, acrostic, rhyme, etc.).
- **Acronym**: word from first letters (e.g., NASA).
- **Acrostic**: sentence from first letters (e.g., "Please Excuse My Dear Aunt Sally").
- **Locus of control**: belief about who controls events in your life (internal vs. external).
- **Self-efficacy**: belief in one's ability to succeed.
- **Close reading**: analyzing all elements of a text to infer meaning.
- **Inference**: reading "between the lines"—meaning implied but not stated.
- **Chunking**: grouping information into meaningful units to aid memory.
- **Modality**: the mode/sense through which learning occurs (visual, auditory, kinesthetic).
- **Cramming**: ineffective last-minute studying; brain can only process 5-7 items at a time.

## Formulas / Dates / People to Memorize
- **Atkinson-Shiffrin model (1968)**: Sensory Memory → Short-Term Memory → Long-Term Memory.
- **SQ3R (Francis Pleasant Robinson, 1946)**: Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review.
- **Bloom's Taxonomy**: hierarchy of critical thinking (knowledge → comprehension → application → analysis → synthesis → evaluation).
- **Gardner's 9 Intelligences**: VML-BIPNE (Verbal, Musical, Logical, Bodily, Interpersonal, Intrapersonal, Naturalist, Existential).
- **1-3 hours** study per 1 hour in class (standard rule of thumb).
- **5-7 items**: limit of short-term/working memory capacity.
- **Study time blocks**: 1-2 hours maximum before burnout.

## Likely Exam Questions
1. **Q:** Name Gardner's three originally most-cited intelligences (of 9). **A:** Linguistic, Logical-Mathematical, and Spatial (or any three from the nine).
2. **Q:** What are the three memory stages in the Atkinson-Shiffrin model? **A:** Sensory, Short-Term (Working), and Long-Term Memory.
3. **Q:** What is the difference between internal and external locus of control? **A:** Internal = belief you control your outcomes; external = belief outside forces control them.
4. **Q:** List three educated-guessing strategies for multiple choice tests. **A:** Choose "C", pick the longest answer, avoid "always"/"never" modifiers.
5. **Q:** What are three of the five context clue types? **A:** Definition, restatement/synonym, contrast/antonym, inference, punctuation.
6. **Q:** Why is cramming the night before ineffective? **A:** Brain can only process 5-7 new items at a time and needs consolidation for long-term storage.
7. **Q:** What is an acrostic? Give an example. **A:** A sentence where first letters spell out info to remember, e.g., "Please Excuse My Dear Aunt Sally" for order of operations.
8. **Q:** What percentage of people learn best visually? **A:** Up to 80%.
9. **Q:** What are the 7 components of a world view? **A:** Model, Explanation, Futurology, Values, Action, Knowledge, Building Blocks.
10. **Q:** What three actions should you take during a test? **A:** Scan the test first, start with easy questions, and keep an eye on the clock.
