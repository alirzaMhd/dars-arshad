🧠 What You're Looking At

Welcome! This page visualizes two directed graphs to help you understand the concept of semiconnectedness.

A directed graph is just a collection of vertices (the numbered circles, 0–4) connected by edges (the arrows). An arrow u → v means you can travel from u to v in one step.

A path u ⇝ v means you can follow arrows from u to v in multiple steps (like 0→1→2 is a path from 0 to 2).

🌟 The Big Question:

For every pair of vertices (u, v), can u reach v OR can v reach u? If yes, the graph is semiconnected.

Think of it like a tournament bracket: for any two players, one must have a path to victory over the other.

🔬 How We'll Find Out — 3 Steps

Step 1 — Find SCCs: Run Kosaraju's algorithm to find groups of vertices that all reach each other (cycles).

Step 2 — Build Condensation DAG: Shrink each SCC group into one "super-node". The result has no cycles.

Step 3 — Check for Unique Topological Order: If there's exactly one way to order the super-nodes left-to-right, the graph is semiconnected. This means the condensation forms a single chain.

📊 The Two Graphs

Graph A (left) — Chain 0→1→2→3→4 with shortcuts 0→2, 1→3 (orange arcs). No cycles.

Graph B (right) — Edges: 0→1, 1→2, 2→0, 3→4, 4→3. Has two cycles, no arrows between them.
