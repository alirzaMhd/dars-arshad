CARTESIAN PRODUCT

Database Systems — Relational Algebra Complete Guide

1. Definition

The Cartesian Product (also called Cross Product or Cross Join) is a fundamental binary operation in relational algebra that combines tuples from two relations into all possible pairs.

Given two relations R₁ and R₂, their Cartesian product R₃ = R₁ × R₂ produces a new relation where:

  • Each tuple from R₁ is paired with every tuple from R₂
  • If R₁ has m tuples and R₂ has n tuples, R₃ will have m × n tuples
  • The schema of R₃ is the concatenation of R₁'s schema and R₂'s schema
Important: The Cartesian Product is the most expensive operation in relational algebra — it can produce extremely large result sets. A relation with 10,000 tuples crossed with another 10,000 tuples produces 100,000,000 tuples!

2. How It Works

The operation works by taking the first tuple of R₁ and appending every tuple of R₂ to it, then taking the second tuple of R₁ and appending every tuple of R₂, and so on.

Visual Process
R₁ = {t₁, t₂, ...tm} R₂ = {s₁, s₂, ...sn} R₁ × R₂ = { (t₁,s₁), (t₁,s₂), ..., (t₁,sn), (t₂,s₁), (t₂,s₂), ..., (t₂,sn), ..., (tm,s₁), (tm,s₂), ..., (tm,sn) }

The notation used in relational algebra is:

Notation:
R₃ = R₁ × R₂
The × symbol denotes the Cartesian Product operation

3. Schema of Result

The resulting relation R₃ = R₁ × R₂ has attributes that are the union of attributes from both R₁ and R₂. If there are common attribute names, they must be qualified with the relation name to avoid ambiguity.

Schema Example

If R₁ has schema (a, b) and R₂ has schema (c, d), then:

R₃ schema = (a, b, c, d)

If R₁ has (a, b) and R₂ has (b, c), then:

R₃ schema = (a, R₁.b, R₂.b, c) — need to qualify duplicate names

Core Formulas

Basic Definition:
R₃ = R₁ × R₂
R₃ is the Cartesian product of R₁ and R₂ — combines all tuples from both relations
Degree (Number of Attributes):
deg(R₃) = deg(R₁) + deg(R₂)
The degree of the result equals the sum of degrees of both input relations
Cardinality (Number of Tuples):
card(R₃) = card(R₁) × card(R₂)
The number of tuples equals the product of tuple counts of both relations
Candidate Key:
C.K.(R₃) = C.K.(R₁) + C.K.(R₂)
Candidate key of result is the concatenation of candidate keys from both relations
Key Insight: The degree is additive, but cardinality is multiplicative. This is why Cartesian Product is computationally expensive.

Extended Formulas

Commutativity:
R₁ × R₂ = R₂ × R₁
Cartesian product is commutative — order doesn't matter (but attribute order changes)
Associativity:
(R₁ × R₂) × R₃ = R₁ × (R₂ × R₃)
Cartesian product is associative — can group operations in any order
Multiple Relations:
R₄ = R₁ × R₂ × R₃
Can extend to any number of relations; degree = sum of all degrees

Properties of Cartesian Product

CommutativityR₁ × R₂ = R₂ × R₁Order of relations doesn't matter

Degreedeg(R₃) = deg(R₁) + deg(R₂)Attributes add up

Important Notes:

  • Cartesian product is the foundation for joins — all join operations are Cartesian product + selection
  • It's rarely used directly in practice due to large result sets
  • Common attribute names must be qualified with relation names
  • Empty relations: if either R₁ or R₂ is empty, R₁ × R₂ is also empty

Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Forgetting that cardinality multiplies (not adds)
  • Not qualifying duplicate attribute names
  • Confusing Cartesian Product with Natural Join
  • Using Cartesian Product when a more efficient join would suffice

When to Use Cartesian Product:

  • When you need all possible combinations of two sets
  • As a building block for implementing joins
  • When generating test data or permutations
  • In combination with selection to implement joins
