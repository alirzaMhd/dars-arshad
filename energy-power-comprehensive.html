Energy and Power of Signals

Signals and Systems — Complete Guide for Exam Preparation

Energy of Signals

Energy measures the total "amount" of a signal over all time. It quantifies how much information or intensity a signal carries.

Continuous-Time Energy

Formula:
E_x = ∫_{-∞}^{+∞} |x(t)|² dt
Total energy of continuous-time signal x(t)

Discrete-Time Energy

Formula:
E_x = Σ_{n=-∞}^{+∞} |x[n]|²
Total energy of discrete-time signal x[n]

Energy Over Finite Interval

Formula:
E = ∫_{t₁}^{t₂} |x(t)|² dt
Energy of x(t) between times t₁ and t₂

Magnitude Definition

For complex signals:
|x| = √(Re² + Im²)
Magnitude = square root of sum of squares of real and imaginary parts
If x(t) is real-valued, then |x(t)|² = x²(t), so the formula simplifies.

Key Properties of Energy

Non-negativity
E_x ≥ 0 always
Scaling
E_{ax} = |a|² · E_x
Time-shift
E_{x(t-t₀)} = E_x

Worked Example 1: x(t) = e^(-|t|)

Calculate the energy of x(t) = e^(-|t|)
Step 1: Write the energy formula
E_x = ∫_{-∞}^{+∞} |e^(-|t|)|² dt = ∫_{-∞}^{+∞} e^(-2|t|) dt
Step 2: Split integral using symmetry
E_x = 2 ∫_{0}^{+∞} e^(-2t) dt
Step 3: Evaluate the integral
E_x = 2 · [-½ e^(-2t)]₀^∞ = 2 · (0 - (-½)) = 2 · ½ = 1
Result:
E_x = 1 (finite, so this is an energy signal)

Worked Example 2: x(t) = rect(t)

Calculate the energy of x(t) = rect(t) (unit rectangle, width 1)
Step 1: Define the signal
x(t) = 1 for |t| ≤ ½, and 0 otherwise
Step 2: Apply energy formula
E_x = ∫_{-½}^{½} (1)² dt = ∫_{-½}^{½} 1 dt = 1
Result:
E_x = 1

Worked Example 3: x[n] = (0.5)^n u[n]

Calculate the energy of x[n] = (0.5)^n u[n]
Step 1: Apply discrete energy formula
E_x = Σ_{n=0}^{∞} |(0.5)^n|² = Σ_{n=0}^{∞} (0.25)^n
Step 2: Recognize geometric series with |r| = 0.25 < 1
E_x = 1/(1 - 0.25) = 1/0.75 = 4/3
Result:
E_x = 4/3 ≈ 1.333 (energy signal)

Worked Example 4: x(t) = e^(jωt)

Calculate the energy of x(t) = e^(jωt)
Step 1: Compute |x(t)|²
|e^(jωt)|² = e^(jωt) · e^(-jωt) = e^0 = 1
Step 2: Apply energy formula
E_x = ∫_{-∞}^{+∞} 1 dt = ∞
Result:
E_x = ∞ (infinite energy — not an energy signal)

Worked Example 5: x[n] = δ[n]

Calculate the energy of x[n] = δ[n] (unit impulse)
Step 1: Apply formula
E_x = Σ_{n=-∞}^{∞} |δ[n]|² = |δ[0]|² = 1² = 1
Result:
E_x = 1

Power of Signals

Power measures the average rate at which energy is delivered. It tells you how "strong" a signal is on average over long time.

Continuous-Time Power

Formula:
P_x = lim_{T→∞} (1/2T) ∫_{-T}^{T} |x(t)|² dt
Average power of continuous-time signal x(t)

Discrete-Time Power

Formula:
P_x = lim_{N→∞} (1/(2N+1)) Σ_{n=-N}^{N} |x[n]|²
Average power of discrete-time signal x[n]

Key Properties of Power

Non-negativity
P_x ≥ 0 always
Scaling
P_{ax} = |a|² · P_x
Time-shift
P_{x(t-t₀)} = P_x

Worked Example 1: x(t) = cos(2πt)

Calculate the power of x(t) = cos(2πt)
Step 1: Apply power formula
P_x = lim_{T→∞} (1/2T) ∫_{-T}^{T} cos²(2πt) dt
Step 2: Use identity cos²(θ) = ½(1 + cos(2θ))
P_x = lim_{T→∞} (1/2T) ∫_{-T}^{T} ½(1 + cos(4πt)) dt
Step 3: The cosine term averages to zero over long intervals
P_x = lim_{T→∞} (1/2T) · ½ · 2T = ½
Result:
P_x = ½ = 0.5 (power signal)

Worked Example 2: x[n] = 2 (constant)

Calculate the power of x[n] = 2
Step 1: Apply formula
P_x = lim_{N→∞} (1/(2N+1)) Σ_{n=-N}^{N} |2|² = lim_{N→∞} (1/(2N+1)) · 4(2N+1)
Result:
P_x = 4 (power signal)

Worked Example 3: x(t) = u(t) (unit step)

Calculate the power of x(t) = u(t)
Step 1: Apply formula
P_x = lim_{T→∞} (1/2T) ∫_{-T}^{T} u²(t) dt = lim_{T→∞} (1/2T) ∫_{0}^{T} 1 dt
Step 2: Evaluate
P_x = lim_{T→∞} (1/2T) · T = ½
Result:
P_x = ½ (power signal)

Worked Example 4: x(t) = e^(-|t|)

Calculate the power of x(t) = e^(-|t|)
Step 1: Apply formula
P_x = lim_{T→∞} (1/2T) ∫_{-T}^{T} e^(-2|t|) dt
Step 2: The integral is bounded (we found E_x = 1 earlier)
P_x = lim_{T→∞} (1/2T) · (bounded value) = 0
Result:
P_x = 0 (energy signal, not power signal)

Worked Example 5: x[n] = (0.5)^n u[n]

Calculate the power of x[n] = (0.5)^n u[n]
Step 1: Apply formula
P_x = lim_{N→∞} (1/(2N+1)) Σ_{n=0}^{N} (0.25)^n
Step 2: Sum is bounded by 4/3 (geometric series sum)
P_x = lim_{N→∞} (1/(2N+1)) · (bounded sum) = 0
Result:
P_x = 0 (energy signal)
Important: If a signal has finite energy (E_x < ∞), its power is always zero. Energy signals are never power signals.

Signal Categories

Signals can be classified based on their energy and power values into three categories.

Energy Signals

Definition
E_x is finite (0 < E_x < ∞)
Power
P_x = 0
Duration
Usually finite or decaying
Examples: e^(-|t|), rect(t), (0.5)^n u[n], δ(t), δ[n]

Power Signals

Definition
P_x is finite and nonzero (0 < P_x < ∞)
Energy
E_x = ∞
Duration
Usually infinite (periodic or constant)
Examples: cos(2πt), u(t), constant signals, periodic signals

Neither Energy nor Power

Definition
Both E_x = ∞ and P_x = ∞
Growth
Signal grows without bound
Examples: t·u(t), e^t, t²

Comparison Table

Category Energy (E_x) Power (P_x) Examples
Energy Signal 0 < E_x < ∞ P_x = 0 e^(-|t|), rect(t), δ[n]
Power Signal E_x = ∞ 0 < P_x < ∞ cos(2πt), u(t), 2
Neither E_x = ∞ P_x = ∞ t·u(t), e^t

Key Relationships

Important:
If E_x < ∞, then P_x = 0
Finite energy implies zero power
Important:
If 0 < P_x < ∞, then E_x = ∞
Finite nonzero power implies infinite energy
Common Exam Trap: A signal cannot be both an energy signal and a power signal at the same time. If E_x is finite, P_x must be zero, and vice versa.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Energy of x(t) = 3e^(-2t) u(t)

Calculate the energy
Step 1: Apply formula with limits 0 to ∞ (due to u(t))
E_x = ∫₀^∞ |3e^(-2t)|² dt = 9 ∫₀^∞ e^(-4t) dt
Step 2: Evaluate integral
E_x = 9 · [-¼ e^(-4t)]₀^∞ = 9 · (0 - (-¼)) = 9/4 = 2.25
Result:
E_x = 9/4 = 2.25 (energy signal)

Example 2: Power of x(t) = 5cos(100πt)

Calculate the power
Step 1: For A·cos(ωt), power = A²/2
P_x = 5²/2 = 25/2 = 12.5
Result:
P_x = 12.5 (power signal)

Example 3: Energy of x[n] = 2δ[n] - δ[n-1]

Calculate the energy
Step 1: Apply discrete formula
E_x = |2|² + |-1|² = 4 + 1 = 5
Result:
E_x = 5 (energy signal)

Example 4: Classify x(t) = e^t u(t)

Determine if this is energy, power, or neither
Step 1: Check energy
E_x = ∫₀^∞ e^(2t) dt = [½ e^(2t)]₀^∞ = ∞
Step 2: Check power
P_x = lim_{T→∞} (1/2T) ∫₀^T e^(2t) dt = lim_{T→∞} (1/2T) · ½(e^(2T) - 1) = ∞
Result:
Neither energy nor power signal (both are infinite)

Example 5: Energy of x(t) = rect(t/2)

Calculate the energy of rectangle with width 2
Step 1: Signal is 1 for |t| ≤ 1, 0 otherwise
E_x = ∫_{-1}^{1} (1)² dt = 2
Result:
E_x = 2 (energy signal)
Common Mistakes:
  • Forgetting to square the magnitude: use |x(t)|², not |x(t)|
  • Wrong limits of integration: always check where the signal is nonzero
  • Confusing energy and power formulas: energy integrates |x|², power averages it
  • Not checking convergence: if integral diverges, energy is infinite
