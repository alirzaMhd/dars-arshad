ATTENUATION

Physical Layer — Complete Guide for Exam

What is Attenuation?

Attenuation is the loss of signal strength (amplitude) as it travels through a transmission medium. It is a natural phenomenon that occurs in all communication systems — the signal gets weaker the farther it travels.

Simple Definition:
Attenuation = reduction in signal power over distance
Measured in decibels (dB). A positive dB means signal gain; negative dB means signal loss (attenuation).

Why Does Attenuation Occur?

Distance

• Signal weakens with every meter traveled

• Longer cables = more attenuation

• Exponential decay in power

Medium Properties

• Copper absorbs energy as heat

• Fiber scatters and absorbs light

• Impurities increase loss

Frequency

• Higher frequencies attenuate more

• Skin effect in copper

• Dispersion in fiber

Other Factors

• Connectors and splices

• Bends in cable

• Environmental interference

How to Combat Attenuation

Amplifiers

• Used for analog signals

• Boost signal amplitude

• Also amplify noise

• Used in long-distance analog links

Repeaters

• Used for digital signals

• Regenerate clean signal

• Do NOT amplify noise

• Used in Ethernet, fiber networks

⚠ Key Difference: Amplifiers boost the entire signal including noise. Repeaters regenerate a fresh copy of the digital signal, removing accumulated noise. Repeaters are preferred in digital communication.

Signal Recovery Chain:
Tx → Cable → Attenuated Signal → Amplifier/Repeater → Restored Signal → Rx
Repeaters/amplifiers are placed at intervals to maintain signal quality over long distances.

The Decibel (dB) Formula

Attenuation in dB (Power Ratio):
dB = 10 × log₁₀(P_out / P_in)
P_out = output power, P_in = input power. When P_out < P_in, dB is negative (loss). When P_out > P_in, dB is positive (gain).
Attenuation in dB (Voltage Ratio):
dB = 20 × log₁₀(V_out / V_in)
V_out = output voltage, V_in = input voltage. Use 20 instead of 10 when working with voltage (assuming equal impedances).

⚠ Critical: When the problem says "signal drops from X to Y", P_in is the original and P_out is the remaining. The result will be negative, meaning loss. If asked for "attenuation" as a positive value, take the absolute value.

dBm Formula (Absolute Power)

Power in dBm:
dBm = 10 × log₁₀(P / 1 mW)
Reference: 0 dBm = 1 mW. dBm measures absolute power level relative to 1 milliwatt.
Attenuation from dBm values:
Attenuation (dB) = P_in (dBm) − P_out (dBm)
Subtract output from input. Positive result = loss. Negative result = gain.

Power Conversion Formulas

Convert dB to ratio:
P_out / P_in = 10^(dB/10)
To find power ratio from dB, raise 10 to the power of (dB/10).
Convert dBm to mW:
P (mW) = 10^(dBm/10)
To find actual power in mW from dBm value.
Convert mW to dBm:
dBm = 10 × log₁₀(P in mW)
To find dBm from actual power in mW.

Chain of Attenuation

Total Attenuation (cascaded segments):
Total dB = dB₁ + dB₂ + dB₃ + ...
When signal passes through multiple segments, add individual dB values. Negative dB values = more loss.
Total Attenuation (with gain):
Total dB = Σ(losses in dB) − Σ(gains in dB)
Losses are negative dB, gains are positive dB. Net result is the overall change.

⚠ dB values add, but power ratios multiply! You cannot add dB values and get the same as multiplying the ratios. Always convert ratios to dB first, add them, then convert back if needed.

Worked Examples — dB Calculations

Example 1: Basic Attenuation Calculation

Problem: A signal enters a cable at 100 mW and exits at 5 mW. Calculate the attenuation in dB.

Given: P_in = 100 mW, P_out = 5 mW

Formula: dB = 10 × log₁₀(P_out / P_in)

Step 1: P_out / P_in = 5 / 100 = 0.05
Step 2: log₁₀(0.05) = log₁₀(5 × 10⁻²) = log₁₀(5) + log₁₀(10⁻²) = 0.699 − 2 = −1.301
Step 3: dB = 10 × (−1.301) = −13.01 dB

The signal lost 13.01 dB of power (attenuation = 13.01 dB).

Example 2: Signal Gain (Amplifier)

Problem: An amplifier takes a 2 mW signal and outputs 200 mW. Calculate the gain in dB.

Given: P_in = 2 mW, P_out = 200 mW

Formula: dB = 10 × log₁₀(P_out / P_in)

Step 1: P_out / P_in = 200 / 2 = 100
Step 2: log₁₀(100) = 2
Step 3: dB = 10 × 2 = +20 dB

The amplifier provides 20 dB of gain (100× power increase).

Example 3: Cable Loss with dBm

Problem: A signal at −3 dBm enters a 500m cable and arrives at −18 dBm. What is the cable's attenuation?

Given: P_in = −3 dBm, P_out = −18 dBm

Formula: Attenuation = P_in (dBm) − P_out (dBm)

Step 1: Attenuation = (−3) − (−18)
Step 2: Attenuation = −3 + 18 = 15 dB

The cable causes 15 dB of loss over 500 meters.

Example 4: Attenuation per Unit Length

Problem: A 2 km fiber link has a total attenuation of 6 dB. What is the loss per km?

Given: Total attenuation = 6 dB, Length = 2 km

Formula: Loss per km = Total attenuation / Length

Step 1: Loss per km = 6 dB / 2 km
Step 2: Loss per km = 3 dB/km

Each kilometer of this fiber loses 3 dB (signal halves every km).

Example 5: Cascaded Attenuation

Problem: A signal passes through: Cable A (−5 dB), Connector (−1 dB), Cable B (−8 dB), Repeater (+12 dB), Cable C (−4 dB). Find total gain/loss.

Given: Multiple segments with gains and losses

Formula: Total dB = Sum of all dB values

Step 1: List all values: −5, −1, −8, +12, −4
Step 2: Sum = (−5) + (−1) + (−8) + 12 + (−4)
Step 3: Sum = −5 − 1 − 8 + 12 − 4 = −6 dB

Net loss is 6 dB. The signal arrives at half its original power.

Detailed Solutions & Explanations

Solution 1: Understanding the Negative Sign

Why is dB negative for attenuation?

When P_out < P_in, the ratio P_out/P_in is less than 1, so log₁₀(ratio) is negative, making dB negative.

Key Insight:
Negative dB = Signal Loss (Attenuation)
In exam questions, if asked "what is the attenuation", give the positive magnitude. If asked "what is the dB value", give the negative number.

Solution 2: dBm to dB Conversion

Common Exam Trap: Confusing dB with dBm

Remember:
dB = relative (ratio), dBm = absolute (power level)
dBm is referenced to 1 mW. dB is a ratio between two values. To find attenuation, subtract dBm values: Attenuation = P_in(dBm) − P_out(dBm)

Example: If input = 10 dBm and output = −5 dBm:

Attenuation = 10 − (−5) = 15 dB
Solution 3: Converting dB Back to Power Ratio

Problem: A signal has −20 dB loss. What fraction of power remains?

Step 1: Use: P_out/P_in = 10^(dB/10)
Step 2: Ratio = 10^(−20/10) = 10^(−2) = 0.01
Step 3: Only 1% of original power remains
Shortcut:
−20 dB = ÷100 = 1% remaining
Every −10 dB removes 90% of power. Two −10 dB stages = 1% remaining.
Solution 4: Maximum Cable Length Problem

Problem: A transmitter outputs 0 dBm. The receiver needs at least −30 dBm. Cable attenuation is 5 dB/100m. What is the max cable length?

Step 1: Available budget = P_in − P_min = 0 − (−30) = 30 dB
Step 2: Max length = Budget / Attenuation per unit
Step 3: Max length = 30 dB / (5 dB/100m) = 600 meters
Link Budget Formula:
Max Length = (P_tx − P_rx_min) / Attenuation_per_unit
This is commonly used in network design to determine maximum cable runs.
Solution 5: Cascaded System with Repeater

Problem: A 10 km link has cable attenuation of 4 dB/km. A repeater provides +25 dB gain. Input is 5 dBm. What is the output power?

Step 1: Total cable loss = 4 dB/km × 10 km = 40 dB
Step 2: Net change = +25 dB (repeater) − 40 dB (cable) = −15 dB
Step 3: Output = Input + Net change = 5 + (−15) = −10 dBm
Important:
dBm_out = dBm_in + Gain(dB) − Loss(dB)
The repeater cannot fully compensate for the cable loss in this case. Signal is 15 dB weaker at the output.

Amplifier vs Repeater

Amplifier

• Analog devices

• Boosts signal + noise

• Used in: TV, radio, phone

• Simple amplification

• Risk: noise accumulates

Repeater

• Digital devices

• Regenerates clean signal

• Used in: Ethernet, fiber

• Detects & regenerates bits

• Eliminates noise

Exam Cheat Sheet

Problem-Solving Steps

1.Identify: What values are given? (mW, dBm, dB)
2.Convert to same unit if needed
3.Apply correct formula (power vs voltage)
4.Negative dB = loss, positive dB = gain
5.For cascaded systems: add all dB values

Common Exam Traps

✗Using 20× instead of 10× for power
✗Confusing dB with dBm
✗Subtracting wrong order in dBm
✗Forgetting log₁₀(2) ≈ 0.3
✗Mixing up amplifier and repeater
