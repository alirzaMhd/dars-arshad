PROPAGATION DELAY

Physical Layer — Complete Guide for Exam

What is Propagation Delay?

Propagation delay is the time required for a signal to travel from one end of a link to the other. It depends on two factors:

Distance
Length of the medium (meters)

The longer the cable, the more time the signal needs to travel.

Propagation Speed
Speed in the medium (m/s)

Different materials allow signals to travel at different speeds.

Key Points

  • Propagation delay is NOT about how fast data is sent
  • It's about how long the signal takes to physically reach the other end
  • Even at light speed, signals take time to travel long distances
  • Example: Light takes ~13ms to travel from Earth to Moon (384,400 km)
Common Mistake: Students often confuse propagation delay with transmission delay. Propagation delay = travel time. Transmission delay = time to push all bits onto the link.

Core Formula

Propagation Delay Formula
Tpd = d / v
or equivalently: D = Distance / V

Variable Definitions

Symbol Name Unit Description
Tpd Propagation Delay seconds (s) Time for signal to travel from source to destination
d Distance meters (m) Length of the transmission medium
v Propagation Speed m/s Speed at which signal travels in the medium

Rearranged Formulas

Find Distance
d = Tpd × v
Find Speed
v = d / Tpd

Important Notes

  • The formula assumes constant speed in the medium
  • Speed is typically a fraction of the speed of light in vacuum (c = 3×10⁸ m/s)
  • Speed factor (k) = v/c, where 0 < k < 1

Worked Examples

Example 1: Basic Copper Cable Calculation

A signal travels through a 1500m copper cable. The propagation speed in copper is 2.3 × 10⁸ m/s. Find the propagation delay.

Given: d = 1500 m, v = 2.3 × 10⁸ m/s
Formula: Tpd = d / v
Calculation: Tpd = 1500 / (2.3 × 10⁸)
Simplify: Tpd = 1500 / 230,000,000
Tpd = 6.52 × 10⁻⁶ s = 6.52 μs (microseconds)
Example 2: Fiber Optic Cable

A fiber optic cable is 2000m long. The propagation speed in fiber is 2.0 × 10⁸ m/s. Find the propagation delay.

Given: d = 2000 m, v = 2.0 × 10⁸ m/s
Formula: Tpd = d / v
Calculation: Tpd = 2000 / (2.0 × 10⁸)
Simplify: Tpd = 2000 / 200,000,000
Tpd = 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ s = 10 μs (microseconds)
Example 3: Finding Distance (Reverse Calculation)

The propagation delay is 0.005 seconds and the speed is 2 × 10⁸ m/s. Find the distance.

Given: Tpd = 0.005 s, v = 2 × 10⁸ m/s
Formula: d = Tpd × v
Calculation: d = 0.005 × (2 × 10⁸)
Simplify: d = 0.005 × 200,000,000
d = 1,000,000 m = 1000 km
Example 4: Comparing Media (Same Distance)

Compare propagation delay for 5000m in different media:

Medium Speed (m/s) Calculation Delay
Copper 2.3 × 10⁸ 5000 / 2.3×10⁸ 21.74 μs
Fiber 2.0 × 10⁸ 5000 / 2.0×10⁸ 25.00 μs
Free Space 3.0 × 10⁸ 5000 / 3.0×10⁸ 16.67 μs
Fastest: Free Space (light) | Slowest: Fiber optic
Example 5: Multi-Hop Path Total Delay

A signal travels: 1000m copper → 500m fiber → 2000m copper. Calculate total propagation delay.

Hop 1 (Copper): T₁ = 1000 / (2.3×10⁸) = 4.35 μs
Hop 2 (Fiber): T₂ = 500 / (2.0×10⁸) = 2.50 μs
Hop 3 (Copper): T₃ = 2000 / (2.3×10⁸) = 8.70 μs
Total: Ttotal = T₁ + T₂ + T₃
Ttotal = 4.35 + 2.50 + 8.70 = 15.55 μs

Key Insights

  • All physical media transmit signals at a significant fraction of light speed
  • Copper is faster than fiber in terms of propagation speed
  • Fiber is preferred for long distances due to lower attenuation, not speed
  • Wireless (free space) has the highest propagation speed

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Don't confuse with Transmission Delay
Transmission delay = L / R (Packet length / Data rate)
Propagation delay = d / v (Distance / Speed)
2. Watch your units!
Always convert km to m, and ensure speed is in m/s
3. Speed factor is important
v = k × c, where k is the speed factor (0.67 for fiber, 0.77 for copper)

Quick Problem-Solving Steps

  1. Identify what you're given (d, v, or Tpd)
  2. Check units - convert if necessary
  3. Choose the right formula variation
  4. Calculate and convert to appropriate unit (μs or ns)

Exam Tips

  • Memorize the speed values for copper (2.3×10⁸) and fiber (2.0×10⁸)
  • Always show your units in calculations
  • For multi-hop paths, calculate each segment separately then sum
  • Propagation delay is independent of data rate and packet size
