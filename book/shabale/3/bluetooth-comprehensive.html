BLUETOOTH (IEEE 802.15)

Comprehensive Exam Prep Guide - Wireless Personal Area Networks

What is Bluetooth?

Bluetooth is a short-range wireless technology standard for exchanging data over short distances using UHF radio waves in the ISM band, from 2.402 GHz to 2.48 GHz.

Named After
Harald Bluetooth

10th century Danish king who united Danish tribes into a single kingdom

Developed By
Ericsson

Swedish telecommunications company, 1998

IEEE Standard
802.15.1

Personal Area Network (PAN) standard

Exam Key Points
  • Bluetooth = IEEE 802.15.1 standard
  • Personal Area Network (PAN) technology
  • Short-range wireless communication
  • Named after King Harald Bluetooth (unifying metaphor)

Piconet Structure

A piconet is the basic topology of Bluetooth networking, consisting of one master device and up to seven active slave devices.

Piconet Capacity
1 Master + 7 Active Slaves = 8 Total Devices
The master device controls all communication within the piconet

Master/Slave Roles

Role Function Address
Master Controls piconet, initiates communication Unique Bluetooth device address
Slave Responds to master, transmits when addressed 3-bit active member address (0-7)
Addressing Note: Slaves use 3-bit addresses (0-7), but address 0 is typically reserved for broadcast, so effectively 7 active slaves maximum.
Addressing
3-bit address → 2³ = 8 addresses (0-7)
But only 7 active slaves per piconet (master takes one slot)

Scatternet

Multiple piconets can be interconnected to form a scatternet, allowing communication across a larger network.

Scatternet Formation
Piconet A: [Master A] ←→ [Slave 1] ←→ [Slave 2] ↕ (shared device) Piconet B: [Master B] ←→ [Slave 1] ←→ [Slave 3]

A device can participate in multiple piconets by time-sharing its participation.

Interconnection
Bridge nodes connect piconets
Time-sharing
Devices alternate between piconets
Range Extension
Covers larger area than single piconet

Piconet Topology Details

Understanding the internal architecture of a Bluetooth piconet is essential for exam preparation.

Master Device Responsibilities

Responsibility Description
Timing Control Synchronizes all devices in the piconet
Frequency Hopping Determines the hopping sequence for all devices
Address Assignment Assigns 3-bit active member addresses to slaves
Power Control Manages power levels of slave devices
Maximum Active Slaves
2³ - 1 = 7 active slaves
3-bit address field supports 8 values, but master uses one
Total Possible Addresses
2⁸ - 1 = 255 addresses
8-bit device address for identification, but only 7 active at a time

TDM (Time Division Multiplexing)

Bluetooth uses Time Division Multiplexing to share the communication channel among multiple devices within a piconet.

TDM Time Slots
Slot duration = 625 μs
Each slot is dedicated to one device at a time

How TDM Works in Bluetooth

TDM Slot Allocation Example
Time: [0] [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] ... M→S1 S1→M M→S2 S2→M M→S3 S3→M M→S1 ... ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ Master sends, slave responds in next slot

The master alternates between sending to different slaves, giving each a time slot.

TDM Advantages

Simple Implementation
Easy to schedule and manage
Fair Access
Each device gets equal opportunity
No Collisions
Only one device transmits at a time

Frequency Hopping

Bluetooth employs frequency hopping to avoid interference and improve reliability.

Hopping Rate
1600 hops/second
The frequency changes 1600 times per second
Hop Duration
1/1600 = 625 μs per hop
Each frequency is used for exactly 625 microseconds
Frequency Channels
79 channels in 2.4 GHz ISM band
Each channel is 1 MHz wide, from 2.402 to 2.480 GHz
Hopping Sequence Example
Hop 1: f = 2.402 GHz (channel 0) Hop 2: f = 2.434 GHz (channel 32) Hop 3: f = 2.468 GHz (channel 66) Hop 4: f = 2.415 GHz (channel 13) ... (deterministic pseudo-random sequence)

The hopping sequence is determined by the master's Bluetooth address.

Frequency Band & Modulation

Bluetooth operates in the 2.4 GHz ISM (Industrial, Scientific, Medical) band, which is globally available for unlicensed use.

Frequency Range
2.402 - 2.480 GHz

ISM band, unlicensed worldwide

Channel Width
1 MHz per channel

79 channels total

Modulation
FHSS

Frequency Hopping Spread Spectrum

FHSS Principle
Signal hops across 79 frequencies
Avoids interference by spreading signal across spectrum

Exam Tip: Most Bluetooth headphones, keyboards, and mice are Class 2 devices with ~10 meter range. Class 1 is for industrial applications requiring longer range.

Half-Duplex Operation

Bluetooth operates in half-duplex mode, meaning devices can transmit or receive, but not both simultaneously.

Half-Duplex vs Full-Duplex
Half-Duplex (Bluetooth): Device A → Device B (transmit) Device A ← Device B (receive) (Cannot do both at same time) Full-Duplex (e.g., phone call): Device A ↔ Device B (simultaneous)
TDM in Half-Duplex
Slots alternate: TX → RX → TX → RX
Time slots separate transmission and reception
