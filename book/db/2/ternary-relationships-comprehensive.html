TERNARY RELATIONSHIPS

Three-Entity Associations — Complete Guide for Exam

What is a Ternary Relationship?

Definition:
Ternary Relationship = A relationship that involves three entity types simultaneously
Degree = 3 (three entities participate at once)

Key Points

Notation

Diamond with three lines connecting to three entities

Degree = 3

Meaning

All three entities must be associated at once

Not three separate binary relationships

Attributes

Can have attributes on the relationship

e.g., QTY in Supplier-Part-Project

Classic Example: Supplier-Part-Project

Entities: Supplier, Part, Project

Relationship: Supplier supplies Part to Project

Attribute: QTY (quantity supplied)

The relationship requires all three entities. A supplier supplies a part to a specific project.

Ternary vs Binary Relationships

When to use Ternary vs Multiple Binary

Ternary

When the association requires all three entities at once

Atomic, inseparable association

Multiple Binary

When each pair has independent relationships

Separate, independent associations

Feature Ternary Multiple Binary
Entities 3 at once 2 at a time
Meaning Atomic association Separate associations
Cardinality 3-tuple Pairs
Example Supplier-Part-Project Supplier→Part, Part→Project
Important

Three binary relationships do NOT equal one ternary relationship. They have different meanings!

Cardinality in Ternary Relationships

Types of Cardinality

(1,1,1)

Each entity is at most one

One-to-one-to-one

(1,1,n)

Two entities are one, third is many

One supplier, one part, many projects

(1,m,n)

One entity is one, two are many

One supplier, many parts, many projects

(m,n,p)

All three are many

Many suppliers, many parts, many projects

Reading Cardinality:
For each combination of other entities, how many instances of this entity can participate?
e.g., For a given Part and Project, how many Suppliers can supply?

Participation Constraints

  • Total Participation (double line): Entity MUST participate
  • Partial Participation (single line): Entity MAY NOT participate
  • Each entity can have different participation in ternary

Examples & Conversion

Example 1: Supplier-Part-Project

Entities: Supplier, Part, Project

Relationship: Supplies (ternary)

Attribute: QTY

Cardinality: (1,1,n) - Each supplier supplies each part to at most one project

Relational: Supplies(Supplier_ID, Part_ID, Project_ID, QTY)

Example 2: Doctor-Patient-Medication

Entities: Doctor, Patient, Medication

Relationship: Prescribes (ternary)

Attribute: Dosage, Duration

Relational: Prescribes(Doctor_ID, Patient_ID, Medication_ID, Dosage, Duration)

Example 3: Student-Course-Semester

Entities: Student, Course, Semester

Relationship: Enrolls_In (ternary)

Attribute: Grade

Relational: Enrollment(Student_ID, Course_ID, Semester_ID, Grade)

Example 4: Company-Product-Country

Entities: Company, Product, Country

Relationship: Markets (ternary)

Attribute: Revenue

Relational: Markets(Company_ID, Product_ID, Country_ID, Revenue)

Relational Conversion

Conversion Rule:
Create a table with FKs from ALL three entities + relationship attributes
Primary key = combination of all three FKs (or subset based on cardinality)
Common Mistake

Don't split ternary into three binary relationships. They have different semantics!
