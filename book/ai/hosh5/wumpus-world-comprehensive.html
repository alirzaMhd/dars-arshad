Wumpus World

Artificial Intelligence — Logical Agents Chapter (6.2)

Environment Description

The Wumpus World is a cave consisting of connected rooms arranged in a 4×4 grid. A Wumpus (a dangerous creature) lurks somewhere in the cave. Pits are scattered throughout, and the agent's goal is to find gold while avoiding death.

4×4 grid of rooms
Agent starts at [1,1] facing right. Gold and Wumpus placed uniformly at random. Each non-start cell has probability 1/2 of containing a pit.

Partial Observability: The agent only perceives the current room's sensors. It has no global view of the cave. This makes the Wumpus World a partially observable environment — the agent must maintain a KB to track what it has learned.

Agent Reasoning Process

The KB-agent combines background rules with observations to deduce safe squares, pit locations, and Wumpus locations. Here is the step-by-step reasoning process from the textbook example.

Step 1: Start at [1,1]

Percept: [None, None, None, None, None]

Inference: No stench → Wumpus is not adjacent. No breeze → no pit adjacent.

KB Update: [1,2] and [2,1] are safe (OK). Agent moves to [2,1].

Actions: Forward

Step 2: At [2,1]

Percept: [None, Breeze, None, None, None]

Inference: Breeze → pit in [2,2] or [3,1]. Pit cannot be in [1,1] (already known safe). So pit is in [2,2] or [3,1] (or both).

KB Update: [2,2] is dangerous (P?), [3,1] is dangerous (P?).

Actions: Only [1,2] is still safe and unvisited. Return to [1,1], then go to [1,2].

Step 3: At [1,2]

Percept: [Stench, None, None, None, None]

Inference: Stench → Wumpus is adjacent. Wumpus cannot be in [1,1] (known safe). Wumpus cannot be in [2,2] (no stench was felt at [2,1], so Wumpus not adjacent to [2,1], ruling out [2,2]... wait, actually we need to check: at [2,1] there was no stench, so Wumpus is NOT adjacent to [2,1]. [2,2] is adjacent to [2,1], so Wumpus cannot be at [2,2]. Therefore Wumpus must be at [1,3]!

KB Update: Wumpus at [1,3] (W!). Also: no breeze at [1,2] → no pit at [2,2]. Combined with earlier knowledge (pit at [2,2] or [3,1]), pit must be at [3,1]!

Actions: Go to [2,1] (known safe, proves no pit at [2,2]), then proceed to [2,3] and grab gold.

Step 4: At [2,2] then [2,3]

At [2,2]: Proves safe (no pit). Agent proved this by combining information from [1,1], [2,1], and [1,2].

At [2,3]: Perceives [None, None, Glitter, None, None]. Gold found!

Action: Grab → Game won with score 1000 - 6 = 994 (6 moves × -1 each).

Key Insight: This reasoning combines information from different times and locations. The agent deduces the Wumpus is at [1,3] by combining the stench at [1,2] with the absence of stench at [2,1]. This kind of cross-temporal, cross-spatial inference is what makes logical agents powerful.

Worked Example 1: Proving Safe Squares

Why is [1,2] safe after starting at [1,1]?

KB contains:

1. "If no breeze at [x,y], then no pit in any adjacent cell" 2. "If no stench at [x,y], then no Wumpus in any adjacent cell" 3. "At [1,1]: no breeze, no stench" (from percept at t=0) 4. "[1,2] is adjacent to [1,1]"

Inference:

From 3 and 4: No pit adjacent to [1,1] → no pit at [1,2] From 3 and 4: No Wumpus adjacent to [1,1] → no Wumpus at [1,2] ∴ [1,2] is safe (OK)

Similarly, [2,1] is proven safe by the same logic.

Worked Example 2: Locating the Wumpus

How does the agent know the Wumpus is at [1,3]?
KB at this point: 1. Stench at [1,2] → Wumpus in [1,1], [2,2], or [1,3] 2. No stench at [1,1] → Wumpus NOT in [1,2], [2,1] 3. No stench at [2,1] → Wumpus NOT in [1,1], [2,2], [3,1] 4. [1,1] is safe (Wumpus not there) From 1: candidates are { [1,1], [2,2], [1,3] } From 3: remove [1,1] and [2,2] from candidates ∴ Wumpus must be at [1,3]

This deduction uses the absence of stench at [2,1] to eliminate [2,2] as a candidate. This is negative reasoning — using what is NOT present.

Worked Example 3: Performance Score Calculation

Optimal Agent Play Score

Consider the textbook scenario: Agent starts at [1,1], moves to [2,1], returns to [1,1], moves to [1,2], returns to [1,1], moves to [2,1], then [2,2], then [2,3] (finds gold), then grabs.

Actions performed: 1. Forward [1,1]→[2,1] (-1) 2. Forward [2,1]→[1,1] (-1) [backtrack] 3. Forward [1,1]→[1,2] (-1) 4. Forward [1,2]→[1,1] (-1) [backtrack] 5. Forward [1,1]→[2,1] (-1) 6. Forward [2,1]→[2,2] (-1) 7. Forward [2,2]→[2,3] (-1) 8. Grab (-1) Total moves: 8 × (-1) = -8 Gold bonus: +1000 Final score: 1000 - 8 = 992

If the agent also needed to kill the Wumpus first: -10 for shooting = score would be 1000 - 10 - moves = 1000 - 10 - 10 = 980.

Worked Example 4: Model Counting for Pit Location

Can the agent be sure about pits at [2,2] and [3,1]?

The agent knows: pit is at [2,2] OR [3,1] (from breeze at [2,1]). There are 2³ = 8 possible models for pits in [1,2], [2,2], [3,1].

Models where KB is true (only 3 survive): Model 1: P₁₂=F, P₂₂=F, P₃₁=T → KB ✓, "no pit at [1,2]" ✓ Model 2: P₁₂=F, P₂₂=T, P₃₁=F → KB ✓, "no pit at [1,2]" ✓ Model 3: P₁₂=F, P₂₂=T, P₃₁=T → KB ✓, "no pit at [1,2]" ✓ In ALL models where KB is true, "no pit at [1,2]" is also true. ∴ KB ⊨ "no pit at [1,2]" In 2 of 3 models, "no pit at [2,2]" is FALSE. ∴ KB ⊭ "no pit at [2,2]" (agent cannot conclude this) ∴ KB ⊭ "pit at [2,2]" (agent cannot conclude this either) Agent can only calculate probability, not certainty.

This demonstrates model checking: enumerate all models, check which satisfy KB, then check if the query is true in all of them.

Key Takeaway: The Wumpus World demonstrates why knowledge representation and logical inference are essential. A simple reactive agent would die immediately. Only by maintaining a KB and reasoning about observations can the agent safely navigate and find the gold.
