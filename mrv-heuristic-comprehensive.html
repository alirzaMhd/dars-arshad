MRV — Minimum Remaining Values Heuristic

Choose the Most Constrained Variable First (Fail-First Heuristic)

What is MRV?

MRV (Minimum Remaining Values) is a heuristic for choosing the next variable to assign in backtracking search. It selects the variable with the fewest legal values remaining in its domain.

Also Known As

  • • Most Constrained Variable — the variable that is hardest to satisfy
  • • Fail-First Heuristic — because it's likely to cause failure early, pruning the search tree
Selection Criterion:
var = argminXi |Di — eliminated values|
Choose the variable with the smallest remaining domain size

Why Fail-First Works

At first glance, it seems counterintuitive: why choose a variable that's likely to fail? The reasoning:

  • 1. If a variable will eventually fail, it's better to discover this early
  • 2. Early failure = smaller search tree = less backtracking
  • 3. All paths lead to the same solution; pruning early saves work
Intuition: "If you're going to fail, fail fast!" A failure at a shallow node prunes an entire subtree. Finding dead ends early is exponentially beneficial.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Australia Map — MRV in Action

Initial state: All 7 variables have domain size 3.

MRV doesn't help initially — all domains are equal. Fall back to degree heuristic.

After WA=red: SA and NT lose red from their domains → both have 2 values remaining, others have 3.

MRV picks SA or NT next (domain size 2 vs 3).

This leads to faster constraint propagation and earlier dead-end detection

Example 4: When MRV Detects Immediate Failure

Partial assignment: WA=red, NT=green, Q=blue

SA's remaining domain: {} (all colors taken by neighbors)

MRV selects SA (domain size 0) and immediately detects failure.

Without MRV: Algorithm might assign other variables first, wasting time.

With MRV: Failure detected immediately → backtrack at the right point.

MRV makes backtracking much more efficient

Quick Reference

MRV Heuristic

• Pick variable with smallest domain

• Also called Most Constrained Variable

• Also called Fail-First heuristic

Why It Works

• Early failure = smaller search tree

• Works well with Forward Checking

• Forward checking efficiently computes domain sizes

Limitations

• Needs tie-breaker when domains equal

• Degree heuristic complements MRV perfectly

• Adds overhead for computing domain sizes
